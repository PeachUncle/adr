# ADR-001: Saga Orchestration vs Choreography for Distributed Transactions

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2025-10-07  
**Context:** Visit booking distributed transactions across customers, vets, and visits services  

## Problem Statement

When creating a visit in the petclinic microservices architecture, we need to coordinate distributed transactions across multiple services:
- **Customer Service**: Validate owner exists and has access
- **Vets Service**: Validate veterinarian availability and reserve slot
- **Visits Service**: Create the visit record

These operations must be atomic from a business perspective while maintaining eventual consistency. We need to handle failures, compensation, and ensure idempotent processing across service boundaries.

## Decision Drivers

- **Data Consistency**: Ensure business invariants are maintained
- **Fault Tolerance**: Handle partial failures gracefully  
- **Observability**: Monitor and troubleshoot distributed transactions
- **Complexity Management**: Balance implementation complexity with maintainability
- **Service Autonomy**: Minimize tight coupling between services
- **Operational Overhead**: Consider monitoring, debugging, and operational complexity

## Options Considered

### Option 1: Saga Orchestration Pattern

**Description**: Centralized coordinator service manages the entire transaction flow, sending commands to participants and handling their responses.

**Implementation**:
```
VisitBookingSagaOrchestrator:
  1. START → ValidateOwnerCommand → Customer Service
  2. OwnerValidatedReply → ValidateVetCommand → Vets Service  
  3. VetValidatedReply → ScheduleVisitCommand → Visits Service
  4. VisitScheduledReply → COMPLETED
  
Compensation Flow:
  FAILURE → CancelVisitCommand → CancelVetReservationCommand → ABORTED
```

**Pros**:
- ✅ **Centralized Control**: Single place to define business process logic
- ✅ **Easy Debugging**: Clear visibility into transaction state and progress
- ✅ **Timeout Management**: Orchestrator can handle timeouts and stuck transactions
- ✅ **Complex Flows**: Can handle sophisticated business rules and conditional logic
- ✅ **Monitoring**: Single point for metrics, health checks, and operational dashboards
- ✅ **Testing**: Easier to unit test business logic in isolation
- ✅ **Error Handling**: Centralized compensation logic

**Cons**:
- ❌ **Single Point of Failure**: Orchestrator becomes critical dependency
- ❌ **Service Coupling**: Participants must understand orchestrator's commands
- ❌ **Operational Overhead**: Additional service to deploy, monitor, and scale

### Option 2: Saga Choreography Pattern

**Description**: Decentralized approach where each service publishes domain events and other services react by listening to these events.

**Implementation**:
```
Event Chain:
  1. VisitBookingRequested → Customer Service validates owner
  2. OwnerValidated → Vets Service validates availability  
  3. VetValidated → Visits Service creates visit
  4. VisitCreated → END
  
Compensation Chain:
  OwnerValidationFailed → VisitBookingFailed
  VetValidationFailed → VisitBookingFailed  
  VisitCreationFailed → VetReservationCancelled → VisitBookingFailed
```

**Pros**:
- ✅ **Service Autonomy**: Services remain decoupled and independently deployable
- ✅ **No Central Coordinator**: Eliminates single point of failure
- ✅ **Event-Driven**: Natural fit for event-sourced architectures
- ✅ **Scalability**: Each service scales independently

**Cons**:
- ❌ **Complex Debugging**: Transaction flow scattered across multiple services
- ❌ **Cyclic Dependencies**: Risk of circular event dependencies
- ❌ **Difficult Testing**: Hard to test complete business flows
- ❌ **Timeout Handling**: No central mechanism for handling stuck transactions
- ❌ **Monitoring Complexity**: Transaction state distributed across services
- ❌ **Business Logic Scattered**: Process knowledge spread across multiple codebases

## Decision

**We choose Saga Orchestration Pattern** for the visit booking distributed transaction.

## Rationale

### Primary Reasons

1. **Business Process Complexity**: Visit booking involves sophisticated validation rules, conditional logic based on veterinarian specialties, appointment scheduling constraints, and customer access controls. Orchestration provides a single place to encode these complex business rules.

2. **Operational Requirements**: Our operations team requires clear visibility into transaction progress, the ability to manually intervene with stuck transactions, and comprehensive monitoring dashboards. Orchestration's centralized state management supports these requirements better.

3. **Error Handling Sophistication**: We need to handle various failure scenarios with different compensation strategies:
   - Owner validation failures (immediate abort)
   - Veterinarian unavailability (try alternative vets)
   - Visit scheduling conflicts (reschedule or compensate)
   
   Orchestration allows us to implement this logic clearly in one place.

4. **Testing and Quality Assurance**: We can unit test the complete business process in isolation, making it easier to verify business rules and edge cases during development.

### Implementation Benefits Realized

- **State Management**: `VisitBookingSaga` entity provides complete audit trail
- **Timeout Handling**: `SagaTimeoutManager` prevents stuck transactions
- **Monitoring**: `SagaManagementController` provides operational visibility
- **Idempotency**: `IdempotencyService` ensures exactly-once processing
- **Compensation**: Centralized compensation logic in orchestrator

### Mitigated Drawbacks

- **Single Point of Failure**: Mitigated by:
  - Persistent saga state survives orchestrator restarts
  - Stateless orchestrator design enables horizontal scaling
  - Health checks and monitoring for operational reliability

- **Service Coupling**: Mitigated by:
  - Generic command/reply interfaces reduce coupling
  - JSON-based messaging allows schema evolution
  - Services can evolve independently within command contracts

## Implementation Architecture

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    Saga Orchestration Layer                     │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  VisitBookingSagaOrchestrator                                   │
│  ├─ State Management (VisitBookingSaga)                         │
│  ├─ Command Routing (SagaMessageSender)                         │
│  ├─ Reply Processing (SagaReplyHandler)                         │
│  ├─ Compensation Logic (CompensationHandler)                    │
│  └─ Timeout Management (SagaTimeoutManager)                     │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                     Message Infrastructure                       │
│  ├─ Kafka Topics (commands/replies)                            │
│  ├─ Idempotency Service                                         │
│  └─ JSON Serialization                                          │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                    Participant Services                         │
│  ├─ Customer Service (ValidateOwnerCommand)                     │
│  ├─ Vets Service (ValidateVetCommand)                          │
│  └─ Visits Service (ScheduleVisitCommand)                      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Consequences

### Positive

- **Operational Excellence**: Clear monitoring, debugging, and manual intervention capabilities
- **Business Logic Centralization**: Single place to maintain complex visit booking rules
- **Testing Confidence**: Comprehensive testing of business processes
- **Fault Tolerance**: Robust handling of failures and timeouts

### Negative

- **Additional Infrastructure**: Need to deploy and monitor orchestrator service
- **Learning Curve**: Team needs to understand saga patterns and compensation logic
- **Command Interface Maintenance**: Need to coordinate command schema changes

## Monitoring and Success Criteria

### Key Metrics
- Saga completion rate (target: >95%)
- Average saga execution time (target: <30 seconds)
- Compensation rate (target: <5%)
- Timeout rate (target: <1%)

### Operational Dashboards
- Real-time saga state distribution
- Failed saga investigation tools
- Compensation action tracking
- Service-level error rates

## Future Considerations

### When to Reconsider Choreography

- If visit booking becomes a simple validation chain without complex logic
- If operational team develops sophisticated distributed tracing capabilities
- If business requirements shift toward event-driven customer notifications

### Hybrid Approach

Consider choreography for:
- Simple notification flows (email confirmations)
- Analytics and reporting events
- Audit logging

Maintain orchestration for:
- Core business transactions
- Complex multi-step processes
- Operations requiring strong consistency

## Appendix: Alternative Patterns Considered

### Two-Phase Commit (2PC)
**Rejected**: Doesn't fit microservices architectures due to distributed locks and coordinator dependency.

### Eventual Consistency with Event Sourcing
**Rejected**: Business stakeholders require stronger consistency guarantees for financial transactions like appointment bookings.

### Manual Compensation
**Rejected**: Not scalable for high-volume appointment booking scenarios.
