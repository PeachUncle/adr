# AWS Cost Estimation ADR

* Status: accepted  
* Deciders: Oihana Elizagoyen, Xabier Mikel Gutierrez, Aimar Uriarte  
* Date: 2025/05/06

## Context and Problem Statement

As part of our migration to AWS, we need to monitor and evaluate the costs associated with our deployed infrastructure. This includes services such as **EC2**, **RDS**, **VPC**, **Load Balancers**, and other related services, which are integral to our application. Understanding these costs helps ensure the sustainability of the solution.

## Decision Drivers

- **Cost efficiency**: Track costs to stay within budget for the migration and ongoing AWS usage.
- **Transparency**: Provide clear understanding of each service’s cost breakdown for better resource planning.
- **Scalability**: Ensure the solution remains cost-effective as it scales.

## Considered Options

- **AWS cost estimation**:
  - Track costs using AWS billing reports and the cost explorer tool.
  - Calculate cost for each service based on the actual usage, as seen in the control panel for services like EC2, RDS, Load Balancers, etc.
  - Ensure that cost calculations consider both direct usage and potential scaling, as traffic or resource demands increase.

## Decision Outcome

Based on the current usage of AWS services in May 2025, we calculated the daily, monthly, and annual costs. Below are the estimated costs per service:

| Service                     | Daily (USD) | Monthly (USD) | Annual (USD) |
|------------------------------|-------------|---------------|--------------|
| Amazon EC2 - Compute         | 0.67        | 20.30         | 243.60       |
| Amazon VPC                   | 0.59        | 17.70         | 212.40       |
| Amazon ELB                   | 0.44        | 13.50         | 162.00       |
| Amazon RDS                   | 0.41        | 12.35         | 148.20       |
| EC2 - Other                  | 0.20        | 6.00          | 72.00        |
| Others                       | 0.002       | 0.05          | 0.60         |
| **Total Estimated Cost**     | **2.31**    | **69.90**     | **838.80**   |

These calculations are based on the first 6 days of May, and the actual costs may vary slightly depending on actual usage patterns and scaling during the remainder of the month.

## Positive Consequences

- **Clear cost visibility**: This breakdown allows us to easily track AWS expenses and optimize costs.
- **Proactive budget management**: We can ensure the AWS costs are kept under control and avoid unexpected overages.
- **Scalability considerations**: The cost calculation anticipates future scaling, giving us a better understanding of how costs will grow as traffic increases.

## Negative Consequences

- **Potential for overage costs**: If we scale rapidly or misconfigure resources, costs could rise significantly.
- **Complexity in cost tracking**: While AWS cost tools are available, interpreting and managing the detailed billing reports can sometimes be complex.

## Annex I: Cost Calculation Methodology

We used the AWS control panel and billing reports to gather the following information for the cost estimates:

- **EC2 - Compute**: Calculated based on instance types, running hours, and usage in the month.
- **VPC**: Based on data transfer, networking, and other related charges.
- **ELB**: Based on data processed and active hours.
- **RDS**: Based on instance hours, storage usage, and data transfer.
- **Other services**: Calculated based on any additional resources or usage.

The estimates are based on current usage trends, with assumptions that the costs will remain relatively stable.
