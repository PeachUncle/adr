# Title: Implementing Architecture Tactics for Fault Tolerance and High Availability in a Distributed Monitoring Network

## Context: The Flood Monitoring and Management System relies on a distributed network of sensors and subsystems to monitor environmental conditions in flood-prone areas. The system must remain operational even when hardware or communication failures occur due to adverse environmental conditions or high data load. This requires an architecture that can mitigate the risks of service interruptions and data loss. To address these challenges, a decision is needed on which fault-tolerance and availability tactics—such as redundancy, failover handling, or data replication—can best support the system’s requirement for continuous and reliable monitoring under various operating conditions.

### Mode: RAG Few-shot

#### Decision
To ensure continuous and reliable operation of the Flood Monitoring and Management System, we will implement the following fault-tolerance and availability tactics:

1. Redundancy: We will deploy redundant sensors in critical locations to ensure that if one sensor fails or loses connectivity, another can continue to provide data. This redundancy will reduce the risk of data loss and maintain the monitoring capability of the system.

2. Data Replication: Sensor data will be replicated across multiple nodes in the distributed network. This means that even if one node becomes unavailable due to hardware or communication failures, the data will still be available from other nodes, ensuring the integrity of the data collected.

3. Failover Handling: We will implement an automatic failover mechanism that can quickly switch operations from a failed component to a backup component, minimizing downtime. This will include monitoring the health of sensors and subsystems in real-time and redirecting tasks in case of failure.

4. Decentralized Communication Protocol: Instead of relying solely on centralized data collection, we will employ a decentralized communication protocol that allows sensors to communicate with each other. This enhances resilience against communication failures by allowing data exchange and processing to continue even when parts of the network are offline.

#### Positive Consequences
- Improved reliability of the monitoring system.
- Enhanced resilience to hardware and communication failures.
- Ensured continuity of data collection in adverse conditions.
- Reduced risk of data loss due to replication and redundancy.
#### Negative Consequences
- Increased complexity in system design and management due to redundancy and failover mechanisms.
- Higher resource usage (e.g., network bandwidth and storage) due to data replication.
- The need for ongoing monitoring and maintenance of redundant systems to ensure they function correctly when needed.

#### Rating 4/5
