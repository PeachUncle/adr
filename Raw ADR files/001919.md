# Members and Roles in the Team

* Status: proposed
* Deciders: Oihana Elizagoyen, Xabier Mikel Gutierrez and Aimar Uriarte
* Date: 2025/02/16

## Context and Problem Statement

We need to define the roles and responsibilities of the team members to ensure a proper collaboration and communication in the development of the project.

## Decision Drivers

* **Project Scope & Complexity** – Ensuring the team structure supports the project's technical and functional needs.
* **Skillset & Expertise** – Assigning roles based on individual strengths and expertise.
* **Collaboration & Communication** – Facilitating clear communication and workflow between team members.

## Considered Options

* **Roles based on expertise**
  * Each team member is assigned a role based on their expertise and skillset.
* **Task based assignments**
  * Instead of assigning roles, tasks are assigned dynamically based on the projects needs and team members availability.
* **Self-Organizing Teams**
  * Team members choose their own roles and tasks based on interest, expertise, and project needs.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: "Self-Organizing Teams", the main reason for this is that our team is very small and the project is not very complex. This option facilitates the development of the project in a more flexible way, allowing team members to work on different tasks and areas of the project as needed.

### Positive Consequences

* Encourages ownership and autonomy among team members.
* Team members are not limited to a specific role, allowing them to work on different areas of the project.
* Team members are no always available, so this option allows to assign tasks based on availability.

### Negative Consequences

* Requires a high level of trust and proactive communication among team members.
* Can result in conflicts or inefficiencies without clear guidelines.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### Roles based on expertise

Each team member is assigned a specific role based on their expertise (e.g., Frontend Developer, Backend Developer, UX Designer, QA Engineer).

* Good, because it has clear responsibility and expertise in specific domains.
* Good, because it has structured and efficient workflow.
* Bad, because has limited flexibility if a team member is unavailable

### Task based assignments

Instead of fixed roles, tasks are assigned dynamically based on project needs and team members availability.

* Good, because offers high flexibility, enabling team members to work across multiple areas. Adapts easily to changing project needs and team availability.
* Good, because encourages skill development by allowing members to take on different tasks on different areas of the project.
* Bad, because the lack of clear roles may lead to confusion.
* Bad, because team members have to also create and develop issues and tasks, which can be time consuming.

### Self Organizing Teams

Team members choose their own roles and tasks based on interest, expertise, and project needs.

* Good, because encourages ownership and autonomy among team members.
* Good, because increases motivation and engagement by allowing members to work on tasks they are interested in.
* Good, because allows the team to adapt to team members availability.
* Bad, because can result in conflicts or inefficiencies without clear guidelines.
* Bad, because requires a high level of trust and proactive communication among team members.
