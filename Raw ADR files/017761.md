# ADR-003: Saga Pattern - Orchestration vs Choreography for Distributed Transactions

## Status
**ACCEPTED**

## Context

The Spring PetClinic microservices application needs to handle distributed transactions across multiple services (customers, vets, visits) when creating appointments. The system requires coordination between these services while maintaining eventual consistency and handling failure scenarios gracefully.

Two primary approaches exist for implementing the Saga pattern:

1. **Orchestration**: A central coordinator (orchestrator) manages the saga workflow and explicitly controls the sequence of operations
2. **Choreography**: Services coordinate through events without a central controller, with each service knowing what to do when certain events occur

### Current System Architecture

- **Customers Service**: Manages customer and pet data
- **Vets Service**: Manages veterinarian information and availability
- **Visits Service**: Manages appointment scheduling and visit records
- **Communication**: Kafka-based messaging between services
- **Data Consistency**: Each service has its own database

### Requirements

- Handle complex business workflows across multiple services
- Ensure data consistency despite potential failures
- Provide clear audit trails and monitoring capabilities
- Support compensation (rollback) mechanisms
- Maintain system resilience and fault tolerance
- Enable easy testing and debugging
- Support future workflow complexity growth

## Decision

We have decided to implement the **Orchestration** approach for the Saga pattern.

## Rationale

### Orchestration Benefits

#### 1. **Centralized Control and Visibility**
- **Clear Workflow Definition**: The orchestrator explicitly defines the workflow steps, making it easy to understand the business process
- **Centralized Monitoring**: All saga state and progress can be monitored from a single point
- **Audit Trail**: Complete transaction history is maintained in one place
- **Easier Debugging**: Issues can be traced through a single coordinator rather than across multiple services

#### 2. **Simplified Error Handling**
- **Centralized Compensation Logic**: The orchestrator manages rollback scenarios and compensating transactions
- **Timeout Management**: Single point of control for handling timeouts and retries
- **Consistent Error Handling**: Standardized approach to handling failures across all workflow steps

#### 3. **Business Logic Clarity**
- **Explicit Dependencies**: Service dependencies and calling sequences are clearly defined
- **Business Rules Centralization**: Complex business logic governing the workflow is contained in the orchestrator
- **Easier Testing**: Workflow logic can be tested independently of individual services

#### 4. **Scalability and Performance**
- **Optimized Execution**: Orchestrator can optimize execution paths and parallel operations
- **Resource Management**: Better control over resource allocation and cleanup
- **State Management**: Efficient handling of saga state and persistence

### Choreography Drawbacks for Our Use Case

#### 1. **Complex Coordination**
- **Implicit Workflows**: Business processes are distributed across services, making them harder to understand and maintain
- **Event Chain Complexity**: Long chains of events can become difficult to trace and debug

#### 2. **Limited Control**
- **No Central Authority**: Difficult to enforce business rules that span multiple services
- **Inconsistent Error Handling**: Each service handles errors independently, leading to inconsistent behavior

#### 3. **Monitoring Challenges**
- **Distributed State**: Saga state is scattered across multiple services
- **Complex Observability**: Monitoring requires aggregating information from multiple services

## Implementation Details

### Core Components

1. **CreateVisitSagaOrchestrator**: Central coordinator managing the visit creation workflow
2. **SagaState**: Enumeration defining all possible saga states and transitions
3. **SagaMessage Types**: Structured messages for inter-service communication
4. **Compensating Actions**: Rollback mechanisms for each workflow step
5. **Idempotency Service**: Ensures messages are processed exactly once
6. **Recovery Service**: Handles timeout detection and saga recovery

### Workflow Steps

```
1. Validate Customer → 2. Validate Vet → 3. Reserve Appointment Slot → 4. Schedule Visit
     ↓ (if failure)        ↓ (if failure)         ↓ (if failure)           ↓ (if failure)
  Compensate Customer ← Compensate Vet ← Release Appointment ← Cancel Visit
```

### Key Design Patterns

- **State Machine**: Clear state transitions with validation
- **Command Pattern**: Structured messages for service operations
- **Retry with Exponential Backoff**: Resilient failure handling
- **Idempotent Operations**: Safe message reprocessing
- **Circuit Breaker**: Protection against cascading failures

## Trade-offs

### Accepted Trade-offs

1. **Single Point of Failure**: The orchestrator becomes a critical component
   - **Mitigation**: High availability deployment, proper monitoring, and quick recovery mechanisms

2. **Centralized Logic**: Business logic is concentrated in the orchestrator
   - **Mitigation**: Well-structured code with clear separation of concerns

3. **Orchestrator Complexity**: As workflows grow, the orchestrator becomes more complex
   - **Mitigation**: Modular design with pluggable workflow definitions

### Benefits Gained

1. **Operational Simplicity**: Easier to operate and maintain
2. **Development Velocity**: Faster to implement and modify workflows
3. **Testing Efficiency**: Centralized testing of business logic
4. **Monitoring Excellence**: Comprehensive visibility into distributed transactions

## Consequences

### Positive Consequences

- **Improved Observability**: Centralized monitoring and logging of distributed transactions
- **Enhanced Reliability**: Better error handling and recovery mechanisms
- **Simplified Development**: Clear workflow definitions and easier testing
- **Business Agility**: Faster implementation of workflow changes and new business rules

### Negative Consequences

- **Orchestrator Dependency**: Services depend on the orchestrator for workflow coordination
- **Potential Bottleneck**: High orchestrator load could impact system performance
- **Increased Complexity**: Additional infrastructure components to maintain

### Mitigation Strategies

1. **High Availability**: Deploy orchestrator with clustering and load balancing
2. **Performance Optimization**: Implement efficient state management and caching
3. **Monitoring and Alerting**: Comprehensive observability for early issue detection
4. **Documentation**: Maintain clear documentation of workflows and decision logic

## Alternatives Considered

### Choreography Approach

**Pros:**
- No single point of failure
- Natural service decoupling
- Event-driven reactive architecture

**Cons:**
- Complex workflow understanding
- Distributed error handling
- Difficult monitoring and debugging
- Business logic scattered across services

**Rejected because:** The complexity of coordination and monitoring outweighed the benefits for our use case.

### Hybrid Approach

**Pros:**
- Combines benefits of both approaches
- Flexibility to choose per workflow

**Cons:**
- Increased system complexity
- Inconsistent patterns across the system
- Higher maintenance overhead

**Rejected because:** Added complexity without clear benefits for our current requirements.

## Future Considerations

1. **Workflow Engine Integration**: Consider adopting a dedicated workflow engine (e.g., Temporal, Zeebe) as workflows become more complex
2. **Event Sourcing Integration**: Enhance the saga pattern with event sourcing for complete audit trails
3. **Cross-Service Transactions**: Evaluate distributed transaction managers for specific use cases requiring stronger consistency
4. **Performance Optimization**: Monitor and optimize orchestrator performance as transaction volume grows
