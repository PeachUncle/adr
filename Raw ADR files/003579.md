# ADR-007: Service Design vs Other Architectures  

## Status  
Status: Approved  

## Context and Problem Statement  
The team needs to decide on an architectural approach for the system that ensures modularity, scalability, and maintainability while meeting the non-functional requirements of **security**, **performance**, and **scalability**. Several architectural styles are under consideration, including monolithic, microservices, and service-oriented architecture (SOA).  

**Problem being solved:**  
- Determining the most suitable architecture for the application.  
- Ensuring the architecture aligns with the system's non-functional requirements and business goals or has a negligible impact on them.  

## Driving Forces and Decision Drivers  
The key factors influencing this decision include:  

- **Scalability:** The architecture must support growth in both user base and system complexity.  
- **Maintainability:** Clear separation of concerns to simplify updates and debugging.  
- **Modularity:** A system that allows independent development and deployment of components.  
- **Performance:** Ensuring the architecture does not introduce significant latency or inefficiencies.  
- **Security:** Ensuring the architecture adheres to best practices for protecting sensitive data and preventing unauthorized access.  
- **Industry Standards:** Leveraging widely adopted and proven architectural principles.  
- **Ease of Integration:** Ensuring the system can easily incorporate external services and APIs.  
- **Future-proofing:** An architecture that can adapt to technological changes and new business needs.  

## Options Considered  

### Option 1: Monolithic Architecture  
A single, unified application where all components are tightly coupled and run as a single process.  

**Pros:**  
- Simple to develop and deploy in early stages.  
- Easier to debug due to centralized codebase.  
- Lower initial development complexity.  

**Cons:**  
- Lack of scalability; difficult to handle high traffic or complex business logic.  
- Harder to maintain as the system grows in size and complexity.  
- Updates require redeploying the entire application, increasing downtime.  

### Option 2: Microservices Architecture  
An architecture where the application is broken into small, independent services that communicate over a network.  

**Pros:**  
- High scalability; services can scale independently.  
- Enables polyglot programming, where different services use different technologies.  
- Decentralized development and deployment for faster iteration cycles.  

**Cons:**  
- High complexity in managing communication between services.  
- Increased latency due to inter-service communication.  
- Requires robust monitoring and orchestration tools.  

### Option 3: Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA)  
A design style that emphasizes the use of services to achieve business functionality. Services are loosely coupled and communicate via well-defined interfaces, often using a API gateway, message bus or similar integration mechanisms.  

**Pros:**  
- Promotes reusability of services across different business domains.  
- Simplifies integration with third-party systems.  
- Supports modularity and independent scalability of services.  
- Aligns well with non-functional requirements like **security**, **performance**, and **scalability** through flexible design.  

**Cons:**  
- May introduce overhead due to service orchestration.  
- Requires well-defined service contracts and governance.  
- Potential for increased latency compared to monolithic architecture.  

## Decision Outcome  
The team has decided to adopt a **Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA)** for the system.  

### Why is this the best option?  
- **Modularity:** SOA allows for the independent development, testing, and deployment of services.  
- **Reusability:** Services can be reused across different projects or domains, improving long-term productivity.  
- **Integration:** SOA simplifies the process of integrating with third-party APIs and legacy systems.  
- **Scalability:** Individual services can scale based on specific needs, optimizing resource usage.  
- **Security:** SOA supports secure service boundaries and enables better control over sensitive data flows.  
- **Industry Adoption:** SOA principles are widely used and supported, ensuring access to best practices and tools.  

### Consequences  

#### Positive Outcomes:  
- Increased flexibility in adding or updating system components.  
- Easier debugging and maintenance due to service modularity.  
- Enhanced scalability, allowing services to grow independently.  
- Improved security through service isolation and well-defined communication interfaces.  

#### Negative Outcomes:  
- Additional complexity in service orchestration and governance.  
- Potential for slightly increased latency in service communication.  
- Initial effort required to define service contracts and implement an effective API gateway or alternative.  

#### What will be affected:  
- **Development Process:** Shift to service-oriented design patterns.  
- **Performance:** Minor latency overhead due to service communication.  
- **Tooling:** Adoption of tools for service orchestration, monitoring, and security.  

### How does this decision affect future systems?  
Future systems will inherit a modular and scalable architecture, reducing effort for new integrations and expansions. The emphasis on service contracts ensures compatibility and adaptability to evolving business needs. A strong focus on security ensures the system is resilient to evolving threats.  

### Mitigation Strategy  
- Utilize an efficient API gateway to minimize communication overhead.  
- Implement robust monitoring to track and address performance bottlenecks.  
- Ensure prior training is given to the AML development team on SOA principles, tools, and security practices.  

## Confirmation  
This decision will be validated by:  
- **Performance Testing:** Ensuring latency remains within acceptable bounds.  
- **Integration Testing:** Verifying that services can seamlessly interact.  
- **Monitoring:** Tracking system performance and detect issues.  
- **Security Reviews:** Conducting regular assessments and pen tests to ensure compliance with best practices.
