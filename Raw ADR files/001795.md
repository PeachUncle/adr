# Title: Exploring Software Patterns for Energy-Efficient Data Transmission in Remote Sensors

## Status: Accepted

## Context: The sensors of Flood Monitoring and Management Systems are installed in remote and hard-to-reach locations with limited power sources. To ensure prolonged sensor operation, the system’s software architecture must consider patterns that support energy efficiency by optimizing data transmission. This requires a design that reduces the frequency of non-critical data transmissions while ensuring that critical updates are transmitted promptly. The architecture must balance responsiveness with energy conservation, prompting a decision on which software patterns can help achieve efficient data handling to extend sensor battery life without compromising system reliability.

### Mode: Zero-shot

#### Decision:

Implement a Hybrid Data Transmission Strategy that combines Event-Driven Architecture (EDA) with a Publish/Subscribe pattern, augmented by an Adaptive Data Rate (ADR) mechanism to optimize energy efficiency for the Flood Monitoring and Management System's sensors.

#### Rationale:
1. Event-Driven Architecture (EDA): This approach allows the system to respond to specific events or thresholds (such as critical water levels) rather than continuously polling the sensors for data. By triggering data transmission only when certain predefined conditions are met (e.g., thresholds, anomalies), we can significantly reduce the frequency of data transmission, thus conserving energy.

2. Publish/Subscribe Pattern: This pattern ensures that only relevant data is transmitted to interested parties. Sensors can "publish" necessary updates based on the severity of the data, while the monitoring system can "subscribe" to critical alerts. By categorizing data into critical and non-critical, we optimize for efficient communication, sending high-priority updates immediately while aggregating less-important data for later transmission.

3. Adaptive Data Rate (ADR): Implementing an ADR mechanism will allow the system to adjust the frequency of data transmission based on battery levels, priority of data, and network conditions. When the battery is low, the system can enter a low-power state, reducing transmission frequency, while still being able to wake up for urgent alerts. Conversely, during favorable conditions, the system can increase frequency to ensure timely data availability.

#### Benefits:
- Energy Conservation: This strategy minimizes unnecessary data transmission, preserving battery life and prolonging the operational lifespan of sensors.
- Responsiveness: By focusing on critical updates and adjusting to operational conditions, the system maintains a balance between energy efficiency and timely responses to emergencies.
- Scalability: The architecture can easily accommodate additional sensors or varying data priorities without requiring major redesigns.
By adopting this hybrid approach, the flood monitoring system will effectively extend sensor battery life while ensuring reliable management of critical flood information.
