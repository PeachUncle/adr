# 2. Adopting Service-Based Architecture
Date: 2024-09-25

## Status
Accepted

## Context
- The PITAS application needs to manage jobInformation assignments, worker pools, scheduling, auctions for external workers, and a credit system.
- The architecture should consider our top 3 driving characteristics: interoperability, workflow, and abstraction.
- Following -ilities are to in the category driving characteristics: elasticity, testability, concurrency and data consistency.

## Decision
We decided to adopt a service-based architecture for the PITAS application, dividing the system into two core functional domains:
- **Internal Job Assignment System:** This system will manage jobInformation scheduling, internal worker assignment, and the Worker List. These services are tightly coupled, as they rely heavily on each other for internal jobInformation management.
- **External Work Searcher System:** This system will manage the Auction House and Credits System for finding and compensating external workers. It operates independently of the internal jobInformation assignment system and only interacts with it when internal workers are unavailable.

Se also the [3. Granularity and Service Separation for PITAS Application](0003-granularity-and-service-separation-for-pitas-application.md) ADR for more details on the separation of services.

This architecture will prioritize interoperability, ensuring the smooth integration of third-party services like auction houses and external worker databases. 
It also supports clear workflow management for end users and maintains abstraction by separating internal and external services.

Both Services will use a different database, as we want to prioritize fault tolerance over simplicity and to make sure that the system can always rely on its database even if the other System crashes.

## Consequences
### Positive Consequences
- **Improved Interoperability:** By separating the external Auction House and Credits System from the internal jobInformation assignment processes, it becomes easier to integrate additional third-party services in the future, as the architecture is designed to facilitate external collaboration.
- **Clear Workflow:** The distinction between internal jobInformation assignment and external worker search ensures that each part of the system has a clear and well-defined workflow. This helps end users understand their responsibilities and reduces confusion in jobInformation management processes.
- **Enhanced Elasticity:** Since the external worker search system can scale independently from the internal jobInformation assignment, the system can respond to changes in demand dynamically, adjusting resources based on the number of active external workers and bids.
- **Concurrency Management:** The architecture supports real-time processing of external bids and jobInformation assignments, ensuring accurate handling of simultaneous requests without performance degradation.
- **Fault Tolerance:** Should the external worker search system experience issues or failures, the internal jobInformation assignment system can continue functioning, reducing the risk of complete system failure.

### Negative Consequences
- **Increased Complexity in Integration:** While separating systems enhances modularity, it introduces complexity in maintaining data consistency and ensuring seamless interactions between the Internal Job Assignment and External Work Searcher systems. Careful design of shared data layers and coordination mechanisms will be required.
- **Potential Challenges with Testability:** Since the internal and external systems interact but are designed to be loosely coupled, end-to-end testing may require additional tooling or orchestration to simulate real-world interactions between the two systems.
- **Higher Resource Utilization:** Maintaining two distinct systems with different scaling needs may result in increased resource usage, especially during times when external workers are not required, but the external system still needs to remain active to handle sudden requests.
- **Latency in Cross-System Communication:** While the separation of systems provides modularity, it may introduce latency during communication between the internal and external systems, particularly when handling urgent jobInformation assignments requiring external workers.
